<p>When I was a kid, I was surprised to see pads displayed on Taiwanese store shelves as a form of customer service, similar to how electronic products are available for customers to examine in Best Buy and Apple stores today. Few people bothered to touch, ruin, or steal the pads due to Japanese-influenced etiquette.</p>

<p>In contrast, pads were hidden away in the US retail experience, but there were 3D simulated television ads showcasing &#x201C;how our pad works better!&#x201D; across brands among other technical product specs provided on the packaging. A cousin who has long aspired to be an entrepreneur once remarked, &#x201C;America&#x2019;s good at marketing&#x2026; they also have the money for it.&#x201D;</p>

<p>Culture, high impact, and high scale decisions are recursively related so we should pay attention to those feedback loops and update our definitions as language evolves over generations along with our creations.</p>
